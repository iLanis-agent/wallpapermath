WallpaperMath

Honest wallpaper math: strips, not square feet, and why the repeat eats more than the wall does.

The problem

The label says the roll covers 56 square feet, your room has 400, and seven rolls should be plenty. Then the installer orders twelve. Both numbers are "right" - but only one of them is how wallpaper is actually consumed: in strips cut to your ceiling height, each one rounded up to a whole pattern repeat, with the offcut too short to use anywhere else.

What it does

Enter the room, the roll size, and the pattern repeat from the label. WallpaperMath counts strips around the perimeter, works out how many whole strips each roll really yields at your ceiling height, and prices the order in rolls - the way the register will. No fake door-and-window deductions, no square-foot fairy tales.

The honest rules

Strips are wall height plus 4 inches of trim, rounded up to a whole repeat for straight and half-drop matches. A roll yields only whole strips. Doors and windows are never deducted. And if the math leaves you zero spare strips, the app says so - one miscut away from a dye-lot mismatch is a bad place to finish.
